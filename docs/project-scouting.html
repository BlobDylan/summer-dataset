Ground rules the data imposes

Your four points, plus six more I found in the paper, its code repository and the related literature. Each idea below is scored against these.

Where the 2,286 units are

single unitsall units (single + multi)

Counted from your local NWB files (units/brain_region, is_single_unit).

Reasonable to expect
  • Who is on screen: person and character identity (amygdala led decoding)
  • Scene, place, indoor vs outdoor (parahippocampal cortex)
  • Event boundaries, cuts and elapsed time (PHC, hippocampus, entorhinal)
  • Gist-level meaning of speech: hippocampal neurons track contextual word embeddings
Not reasonable to expect
  • Phonemes or articulation: no auditory cortex (STG) and no speech motor cortex recorded
  • Low-level visual detail: only 15 lingual-gyrus and 6 fusiform units
  • Verbatim word-by-word text: at best meaning, not wording
  • Anything that needs intent or effort: viewing was passive
Your point 1

Passive viewing

There is no produced behaviour to decode. A "brain-to-text" project here decodes what was seen, heard and understood, like Tang 2023 or mind captioning, not what the patient tried to say, like Willett or Card.

Your point 2

Only medial temporal lobe

Amygdala, hippocampus, entorhinal and parahippocampal cortex cover 96% of units. These are memory and concept structures, so projects should target concepts, scenes, events and meaning.

Your point 3

29 patients, but no shared neurons

Each patient contributes a different, unordered set of 30–137 units. What they do share is the stimulus clock: all 29 watched the same 118,162 analysed frames. That makes hyperalignment and shared latent spaces possible, which is the setup MindEye2 and Tang & Huth 2025 rely on.

Your point 4

Invasive and new, so reproductions are cheap wins

fMRI, MEG and ECoG movie studies have established analyses (intersubject correlation, event HMMs, identity RSA, CLIP alignment) that nobody has run on continuous human single-unit data.

Found · dataset

Spike times only, no field potentials

The NWB files hold spike times and mean waveforms, with no local field potentials or iEEG. That rules out ripples, theta phase and broadband power, including the 2025 movie-ripple results.

Found · dataset

German dub, no transcript

Patients heard the German dub with no subtitles. The only speech labels are tom-speaking and summer-speaking. Any language model feature needs a transcript built from the DVD audio (for example with WhisperX), then checked by hand. NNDb's English word timings for this film help, but come from a different release and language.

Found · resolved, DVD will be bought

The movie isn't included

Frames and audio are not distributed. With the Cine Project 2010 DVD (EAN 4010232049162) and the repo's movie_wrapper, you can rebuild the exact 125,743 frames. That unlocks every idea using CLIP, a vision-language model, audio or a transcript.

Found · paper + eLife review

One viewing, strong autocorrelation

With no repeated trials, there is no noise ceiling. Without temporal gaps between training and test data, label decoding inflates to κ≈0.99, so use the 32 s buffers throughout. Per-patient decoding was weak and variable; the headline results pool all patients.

Found · paper

The cast was deliberately unfamiliar

The film was chosen because German audiences didn't know the actors. Few classic concept cells for characters are likely (45 units for Tom, 38 for Summer), but patients meet the characters during the recording, so learning is observable.

Found · baselines

Numbers to beat

LSTM on the pooled population, Cohen's κ: persons 0.36, indoor/outdoor 0.31, characters 0.23–0.32, cuts 0.18–0.20. The best 500 units reach 0.38 for Summer.

My recommendation · thesis-scale

Roadmap

With a large scope and the DVD available, this is a phased plan rather than a single pick. The phases are ordered because each builds what the next depends on, and each lists a machine-learning angle and a neuroscience angle.

Phase 0Weeks 1–4

Groundwork

Rebuild the exact frames with movie_wrapper. Transcribe the German track with WhisperX and hand-correct it, cross-checking against NNDb's English word timings. Extract CLIP, audio and text features per 80 ms bin, and freeze splits with 32 s buffers. Reproduce the published κ values and the cut responses (#24) as a sanity check.

Output: one feature store that every later phase reuses, which is also the core of a benchmark (#16).

Phase 1Months 1–2

One shared space for 29 brains

Align patients through the movie timeline and decode a held-out patient (#9). Then measure how many minutes of viewing a new patient needs (#10). In parallel, map neurons to CLIP embeddings and retrieve held-out frames (#2).

For ML: cross-patient calibration for BCIs. For neuroscience: how much MTL coding is shared between people. Quick wins that fit alongside: #6, #13, #14, #19.

Phase 2Months 2–5

From embeddings to text

Generate scene captions from the shared space (#1), with language-prior and shuffled-neuron controls. Fit contextual LLM encoding models on the dialogue (#4). That result tells you whether the dialogue route in phase 3 is worth taking.

For ML: open-vocabulary text from single neurons during passive viewing. For neuroscience: whether MTL neurons carry contextual word meaning in a film, not just a podcast.

Phase 3Month 5 onward

Choose the thesis direction

Let phase 2 decide. If #4 finds a dialogue signal, go for dialogue retrieval (#3) and name mentions (#7). If scene meaning dominates, build a spike-token model across patients (#11) and extend it to other human datasets (#8). For a neuroscience thesis, take the nonlinear story (#23) or the fMRI ↔ spikes bridge on the same film (#22).
