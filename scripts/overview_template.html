SUMMER Dataset Atlas

Twenty-nine epilepsy patients with microwires in the medial temporal lobe watched 500 Days of Summer from start to finish. We have the spike times of every neuron recorded during the movie, plus frame-by-frame labels for what was on screen. This page shows what that data looks like, from the patients to the spikes.

1 · Data structure

One NWB file per patient, and what's inside

Each sub-N_ses-subN_ecephys.nwb file (~80 MB) holds everything for one viewing session. Most of the size is the movie labels, which are copied into all 29 files. The shapes below are from sub-1.

2 · Who was recorded

Patients & viewing sessions

The patients were being monitored in hospital for epilepsy surgery. Electrodes were placed only where doctors needed them, so each patient covers a different set of regions and yields a different number of neurons.

3 · Where

Electrodes & brain regions

Each bundle holds 8 microwires. There are 245 bundles with at least one neuron, spread over 1,130 wires. In this page's charts, regions are grouped into amygdala, hippocampus (anterior, middle and posterior), entorhinal cortex, parahippocampal cortex, and "Other" (piriform, perirhinal, fusiform, lingual).

4 · What

The neurons themselves

Firing rates, spike-sorting quality and spike shapes for all 2,286 units. Firing rates here are computed only over the analysis window (36.7 s – 79:23), with pauses removed.

5 · The stimulus

The movie, as 53 labels

The movie itself isn't shared, for copyright reasons. What you get is 53 per-frame labels at 25 fps: who is on screen, whose face is visible, who is speaking, where the scene takes place, and where cuts happen. These labels are the targets for any decoding task, so their structure matters.

6 · Spikes on the movie timeline

Neural activity over the movie

Every spike below has been mapped to movie time, with pauses removed. That's what makes it possible to line up neurons from different patients, who watched on different days, as if they had watched together.

7 · Event-locked activity

How neurons respond to events in the movie

Here each event (a camera cut, Summer appearing, a speaker starting to talk) is treated as a trial, and activity is averaged in the second before and after it. As in the paper, an event only counts if the feature was absent for at least 1 s beforehand. Everything is computed on this page from the NWB files.

8 · Before you train anything

Practical facts for machine learning

Two things shape any model trained on this data: how sparse the spike counts are at a given bin size, and how strongly both labels and neural activity are correlated over time. The second is why the authors recommend a 32-second buffer between training and test data.

How this page was made. scripts/build_overview.py reads all 29 NWB files with h5py, computes every statistic shown, and inlines the results into this file. Movie time for each spike comes from linear interpolation through cleaned_watchlogs. Unless a chart says otherwise, analyses use the authors' window of 36.72–4,763.2 s. Checks against the paper: mean firing rate 1.93 Hz, mean SNR 8.20, ISI violations 0.42%, CV2 1.08, median isolation distance 21.8 (1,397 units), 245 bundles, 2.02 neurons per wire. All match the published values.
